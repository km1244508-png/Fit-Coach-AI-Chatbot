# Weight Loss and Calorie Balance

## Energy balance basics
Weight change is driven mainly by long-term energy balance: eating fewer calories than you burn leads to weight loss, and eating more leads to weight gain. Individual responses vary because of metabolism, hormones, sleep, stress and medical conditions.

## Estimating calorie needs
A commonly used estimate of resting energy needs is the Mifflin-St Jeor equation. For men: BMR = 10 x weight (kg) + 6.25 x height (cm) - 5 x age + 5. For women: BMR = 10 x weight (kg) + 6.25 x height (cm) - 5 x age - 161. Multiply BMR by an activity factor to estimate daily needs: 1.2 (little or no exercise), 1.375 (light exercise 1 to 3 days per week), 1.55 (moderate exercise 3 to 5 days per week), 1.725 (hard exercise 6 to 7 days per week), 1.9 (very hard exercise or a physical job). These are estimates and should be adjusted using real progress over 2 to 3 weeks.

## A sustainable deficit
A moderate deficit of about 300 to 500 calories per day below maintenance is a common starting point. A rough rule is that a 500 kcal daily deficit leads to about 0.5 kg of loss per week, although real results vary. A pace of roughly 0.5 to 1 percent of body weight per week is generally considered sustainable and helps protect muscle.

## Avoid extreme dieting
Very low calorie diets, skipping meals as punishment, or eliminating whole food groups can cause fatigue, muscle loss, nutrient gaps and rebound overeating. Anyone with a history of eating disorders, pregnancy, or a medical condition should work with a doctor or registered dietitian before dieting.

## Tracking progress
Weigh yourself under similar conditions (for example morning, after the toilet) and look at weekly averages instead of daily numbers. Waist measurements, progress photos, strength gains and energy levels are also useful signs of progress.
