# Hydration, Sleep and Recovery

## Hydration
There is no single water target that fits everyone. The European Food Safety Authority sets an adequate total water intake of about 2.0 liters per day for women and 2.5 liters per day for men, which includes water from food and all drinks. Needs rise with heat, sweat and exercise. Drinking regularly through the day and aiming for pale-yellow urine is a simple practical check. Thirst is a reasonable guide for most healthy people.

## Sleep
Adults are generally recommended to sleep at least 7 hours per night, and many do best with 7 to 9 hours. Poor sleep is linked with higher appetite, lower energy, slower recovery and worse training performance. A regular sleep schedule, a dark and cool room, and limiting screens and caffeine late in the day can improve sleep quality.

## Recovery
Muscle repair and strength gains happen between workouts. Rest days, enough protein, enough total calories and adequate sleep all support recovery. Light activity such as walking on rest days is fine.

## Muscle soreness
Mild delayed onset muscle soreness 1 to 3 days after a new workout is normal. Sharp pain, joint swelling, or pain that gets worse during movement is not normal and should be assessed by a medical professional.
