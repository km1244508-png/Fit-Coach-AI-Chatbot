# Protein Intake for Muscle and Health

## Daily protein targets
The general Recommended Dietary Allowance (RDA) for protein is 0.8 grams per kilogram of body weight per day for healthy adults. This is the minimum to prevent deficiency, not necessarily the best amount for people who train. Position stands for exercising people (for example from the International Society of Sports Nutrition) suggest about 1.4 to 2.0 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight per day to support muscle gain and recovery.

## Spreading protein across meals
Eating protein across the day, for example 3 to 5 meals each with roughly 0.25 to 0.4 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight, is a practical way to support muscle protein synthesis. Total daily intake matters more than perfect timing.

## Protein while losing weight
During a calorie deficit, keeping protein toward the higher end of the 1.4 to 2.0 g/kg range helps preserve muscle mass while fat is lost.

## Example protein-rich foods
Eggs (about 6 g protein per large egg), cooked chicken breast (about 31 g per 100 g), cooked lentils/dal (about 9 g per 100 g), yogurt, milk, paneer, fish, chickpeas and tofu are common protein sources. Vegetarians can combine dal, chickpeas, dairy, eggs and soy to meet their targets.

## Kidney and medical conditions
People with kidney disease or other medical conditions should ask their doctor before raising protein intake.
