# Exercise Safety and When to Seek Medical Help

## Stop and get urgent help
Stop exercising and seek emergency medical care for chest pain or pressure, severe shortness of breath, fainting, sudden severe headache, sudden weakness or numbness on one side of the body, or a very irregular or racing heartbeat.

## See a doctor
Book a medical appointment for pain that persists for more than a few days, swelling of a joint, numbness or tingling, pain that wakes you at night, or an injury that does not improve with rest. A coach or chatbot cannot diagnose conditions.

## Normal versus warning signs
Muscle fatigue, a burning sensation in working muscles, and mild soreness are normal. Sharp, stabbing or sudden pain, a popping sound with pain, or pain in a joint is a warning sign to stop that exercise.

## Starting safely
If you are new to exercise, are older, have a chronic condition, are pregnant or have had a recent injury, check with a healthcare professional before starting a new program. Start with lower volume and increase gradually.

## Heat and hydration
In hot weather, reduce intensity, drink fluids, and rest if you feel dizzy, nauseous or confused.
