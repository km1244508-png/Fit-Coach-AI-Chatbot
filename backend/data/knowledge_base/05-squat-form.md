# How to Squat with Good Form

## Setup
Stand with feet about shoulder-width apart, toes turned out slightly (roughly 15 to 30 degrees). Keep the chest proud, eyes forward, and brace your abdominal muscles as if preparing to be lightly punched.

## Movement cues
Start by pushing the hips back and bending the knees at the same time. Let the knees track in line with the toes, in the same direction your feet point. Keep your whole foot on the floor, with weight spread across the heel, big toe and little toe. Lower to a depth you can control with a neutral spine, ideally thighs about parallel to the floor or deeper if mobility allows. Drive up through the mid-foot and keep the chest up.

## Common mistakes
Heels lifting off the floor, knees collapsing inward, rounding the lower back at the bottom, and dropping too fast. If heels lift, try a small plate under the heels or work on ankle mobility. If the knees cave in, reduce the load and think about pushing the knees out.

## Beginner options
Practice bodyweight squats to a box or chair, or goblet squats holding a dumbbell at the chest, before adding heavy weight.

## Pain warning
Stop if you feel sharp pain in the knees, hips or back. Ongoing pain should be assessed by a doctor or physiotherapist.
