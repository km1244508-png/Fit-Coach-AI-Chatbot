# Strength Training Principles

## Progressive overload
Progressive overload means gradually asking your muscles to do more over time, by adding weight, repetitions, sets, or better control, or by shortening rest. It is the main driver of continued strength and muscle gain.

## Weekly frequency
Public health guidelines recommend muscle-strengthening activities that involve all major muscle groups on at least 2 days per week. Training each muscle group about 2 times per week is an effective and practical approach for most beginners and intermediates.

## Sets and repetitions
For muscle growth, many trainees do about 10 to 20 hard sets per muscle group per week. Beginners often make good progress with fewer sets, such as 6 to 10 sets per muscle group per week. Heavier loads of about 1 to 5 repetitions emphasize maximal strength, while sets of about 6 to 12 repetitions are a popular range for muscle growth; muscle growth can occur across a wide range of repetitions when sets are taken close to fatigue.

## Rest between sets
Rest about 2 to 3 minutes between heavy compound sets such as squats and deadlifts, and about 60 to 90 seconds between lighter isolation sets.

## Rest days and deloads
Muscles adapt during recovery. Beginners usually do well with at least one full rest day between sessions that train the same muscles. If strength keeps dropping, sleep is poor and joints ache, consider a lighter week (a deload).

## Beginner full-body structure
A simple 3-day full-body plan can include a squat or leg press pattern, a hinge pattern such as a Romanian deadlift, a horizontal push such as a push-up or bench press, a horizontal pull such as a row, a vertical push or pull, and core work such as a plank.
