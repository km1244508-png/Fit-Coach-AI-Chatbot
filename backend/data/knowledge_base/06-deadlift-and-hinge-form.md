# Deadlift and Hip Hinge Form

## The hip hinge
The hip hinge is bending at the hips while keeping the spine neutral, with only a soft bend in the knees. Practice by standing a short distance from a wall, pushing the hips back to tap the wall, and returning to standing by squeezing the glutes.

## Conventional deadlift setup
Stand with feet about hip-width apart and the bar over the middle of the feet. Hinge down and grip the bar just outside the legs. Bend the knees until the shins touch the bar, lift the chest slightly, and pull the slack out of the bar so your arms are straight and your lats are tight. Keep a neutral spine from head to tailbone.

## The lift
Push the floor away with your legs while keeping the bar close to your body, and stand tall by extending the hips. Do not lean back at the top. Lower the bar by pushing the hips back first, then bending the knees once the bar passes them.

## Common mistakes
Rounding the lower back, jerking the bar off the floor, letting the bar drift away from the legs, and overextending the back at lockout. Start with light weights or a Romanian deadlift with dumbbells to learn the pattern.

## Safety
Stop if you feel sharp back pain, numbness or tingling down the leg. Seek medical advice for persistent symptoms.
